About me

I am a radiologic scientist and medical educator based in Jeddah. I teach in the Department of Radiological Sciences at King Saud bin Abdulaziz University for Health Sciences, where I have worked since 2010, the same year I graduated in diagnostic radiology from King Abdulaziz University with first class honours.

My path has moved between the imaging room and the classroom. In January 2012 I went to the United States and spent a year studying English at the University of Missouri–Columbia. In January 2013 I started a master’s in bio-imaging at Boston University, working with MRI, and graduated in May 2014. That September I moved to Canada, to the University of Alberta in Edmonton, where I spent a year in Dr. Edmond Lou’s scoliosis research group, a field I still research today.

I returned to Saudi Arabia in September 2015, and a year later went back to the United States to begin a PhD in biomedical engineering at George Washington University, where I studied how reliably ultrasound can measure femoral artery pseudoaneurysms. I finished in February 2020, just as the COVID-19 pandemic began, and returned to teaching at KSAU-HS that September.

Since then my research has turned to artificial intelligence. I build deep-learning models that read medical images, and I study whether the students and radiology staff who will use them are ready to trust them. In January 2026 I began the Fellowship in AI Implementation for Education and Scientific Research.

Teaching led me to medical education. I completed a Master of Medical Education in 2024 and served a full four-year term as Assistant Dean of Academic Affairs, leading faculty development and student assessment across the college. In 2025 I became the first female faculty member at the College of Applied Medical Sciences in Jeddah to be promoted to Associate Professor.
